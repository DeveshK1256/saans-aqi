Scientific Methodology & Calculation Specifications

Core Axiom: The LLM explains, code calculates. All exposure, dose, sub-index, CADR decay rates, and emission numbers come strictly from pure, tested physical code in src/domain/.

1. National Air Quality Index (NAQI) — India CPCB Standard

Calculated in strict compliance with the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) 2014 NAQI standard.

I_p = ((I_hi - I_lo) / (BP_hi - BP_lo)) * (C_p - BP_lo) + I_lo

Rules & Verification:

  • Overall NAQI: Evaluated as max(I_p) across all monitored pollutants.
  • Completeness Gate: Requires at least 3 valid pollutant sub-indices, with at least one particulate matter pollutant (PM2.5 or PM10). If fewer than 3 pollutants are available, marked as partial.
  • CPCB Category Bands: Good (0–50), Satisfactory (51–100), Moderate (101–200), Poor (201–300), Very Poor (301–400), Severe (401–500).

2. Single-Zone Indoor Mass Balance Differential Model

Urban Indians spend 70–90% of their daily hours indoors. Indoor air quality is modeled via the standard single-zone mass balance differential equation (Nazaroff, W. W., 2004, Indoor particle dynamics):

lambda = a + k + (Q_p / V)
C_ss = (P * a * C_out + S / V) / lambda
C_in(t + dt) = C_ss + (C_in(t) - C_ss) * exp(-lambda * dt)

Physical Definitions:

  • a: Air exchange rate (h⁻¹), varying from 0.65 h⁻¹ (closed windows) to 2.8 h⁻¹ (open windows).
  • P: Penetration factor of ambient particles through the building envelope (dimensionless, 0.65–0.95).
  • k: Gravitational and electrostatic particle deposition rate (h⁻¹, 0.10–0.45 h⁻¹).
  • Q_p: Clean Air Delivery Rate (CADR) of filtration equipment (m³/h).
  • V: Effective zone volume (m³); e.g., 38 m³ for typical Indian master bedroom.
  • S: Indoor emission source rate (µg/h); e.g., LPG cooking, agarbatti, or mosquito coils.

3. Personal Daily Inhaled Dose Model

Physical particulate mass deposited in human airways is computed by integrating microenvironmental concentrations with activity-specific volumetric inhalation rates (US EPA Exposure Factors Handbook, 2011, Chapter 6):

Dose_m = sum_s [ C_e(t) * IR(m, a) * dt ] (micrograms PM2.5 / day)
Exposure Ratio = Dose_m / Dose_ref

Where Dose_ref represents the daily inhaled dose for the same person at the WHO 2021 24-hour guideline continuous concentration (15 µg/m³).

Uncertainty Bounds: In accordance with scientific integrity standards, single-point false precision is never presented as definitive truth. Every calculation simultaneously evaluates low, central, and high parameter vectors.

4. What-If Simulator & Strict Zero-Change Invariant

The what-if simulator allows families to assess intervention efficacy before purchasing hardware or changing habits:

  • Zero-Change Invariant (Part 3.4): If user overrides match the baseline profile exactly, the engine is mathematically proven to return strictly 0.0 µg (0%) delta dose and 0.0 g community emissions.
  • Two-Way Community Impact (Part 3.5): Switching transit modes (e.g. from petrol two-wheeler to electric metro) calculates community tailpipe CO₂ and PM2.5 mass avoided using official ARAI/CPCB vehicular emission factors.

6. Relatable Communication Units & Mandatory Caveat

Mandatory Scientific Caveat (Part 3.4 & SCI-06):
Cigarette equivalence is derived using Berkeley Earth's empirical conversion rule (22 µg/m³ 24h average PM2.5 ≈ 1 cigarette). This comparison is deployed strictly as an intuitive risk communication metaphor for the public. Ambient urban air pollution differs fundamentally from directly inhaled cigarette smoke in chemical speciation, absence of heavy tobacco pyrolysis products, particle morphology, and physiological deposition mechanics. It must never be presented as a medical diagnosis or clinical prognosis.

Scientific Methodology & Calculation Specifications

Core Axiom: The LLM explains, code calculates. All exposure, dose, sub-index, CADR decay rates, and emission numbers come strictly from pure, tested physical code in src/domain/.

1. National Air Quality Index (NAQI) — India CPCB Standard

Calculated in strict compliance with the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) 2014 standard by linear interpolation within each criteria pollutant breakpoint band:

Equation 1: CPCB Linear Sub-Index Interpolation
$$I_p = \frac{I_{\text{hi}} - I_{\text{lo}}}{BP_{\text{hi}} - BP_{\text{lo}}} \cdot (C_p - BP_{\text{lo}}) + I_{\text{lo}}$$
Ip = Ihi − Ilo BPhi − BPlo × (Cp − BPlo) + Ilo
I_p: Sub-index for specific pollutant p
C_p: Monitored concentration (µg/m³)
BP_hi, BP_lo: Upper and lower concentration breakpoints
I_hi, I_lo: Upper and lower AQI category breakpoints
Equation 2: Overall Composite NAQI
$$\text{NAQI} = \max_{p \in \text{Pollutants}} I_p \quad \text{subject to } |\text{Pollutants}| \ge 3 \text{ and } (\text{PM}_{2.5} \in \text{P} \lor \text{PM}_{10} \in \text{P})$$

CPCB Category Bands: Good (0–50), Satisfactory (51–100), Moderate (101–200), Poor (201–300), Very Poor (301–400), Severe (401–500).

2. Single-Zone Indoor Mass Balance Differential Model

Urban families spend 70–90% of their day indoors. Indoor air quality is modeled using the classical physical mass balance differential equation (Nazaroff, W. W., 2004, Indoor particle dynamics):

Equation 3: Total Particle Removal Decay Rate ($\lambda$)
$$\lambda = a + k + \frac{Q_p}{V}$$
a: Air exchange rate (h⁻¹) [0.65 sealed, 2.8 open]
k: Deposition loss rate (h⁻¹) [0.25 h⁻¹]
Q_p: Purifier Clean Air Delivery Rate (CADR, m³/h)
V: Room air volume (m³) [e.g. 38 m³ bedroom]
Equation 4: Steady-State Indoor Concentration ($C_{ss}$)
$$C_{ss} = \frac{P \cdot a \cdot C_{\text{out}} + \frac{S}{V}}{\lambda}$$
P: Envelope penetration factor (0.65–0.95)
C_out: Ambient outdoor concentration (µg/m³)
S: Indoor emission source rate (µg/h)
S / V: Source concentration injection rate (µg/m³·h)
Equation 5: Transient Indoor Hourly Update
$$C_{\text{in}}(t + \Delta t) = C_{ss} + \big(C_{\text{in}}(t) - C_{ss}\big) \cdot e^{-\lambda \Delta t}$$

This differential solution accurately tracks rapid particulate decay when an air purifier is switched on, or concentration surges during unvented cooking.

3. Personal Daily Inhaled Dose Model

Physical particulate mass deposited in human airways is computed by integrating microenvironmental concentrations with activity-specific volumetric inhalation rates (US EPA Exposure Factors Handbook, 2011, Chapter 6):

Equation 6: 24-Hour Cumulative Inhaled Dose
$$\text{Dose}_m = \sum_{s \in \text{Schedule}} C_e(t) \cdot \text{IR}(m, a) \cdot \Delta t_s \quad [\mu\text{g PM}_{2.5}/\text{day}]$$
m: Household member (child, toddler, adult, elderly)
IR(m, a): Volumetric inhalation rate (m³/h)
C_e(t): Concentration in microenvironment e (µg/m³)
Δt_s: Duration spent in schedule segment s (hours)
Equation 7: Exposure Ratio to WHO Continuous Guideline
$$\text{Exposure Ratio} = \frac{\text{Dose}_m}{\text{Dose}_{\text{ref}}} \quad \text{where } \text{Dose}_{\text{ref}} = 15\,\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3 \times \overline{\text{IR}} \times 24\,\text{h}$$

Evaluated across [low, central, high] parameter vectors to reflect physiological uncertainty.

4. What-If Simulator & Two-Way Community Impact

Equation 8: Personal Avoided Dose & Strict Zero-Change Invariant
$$\Delta \text{Dose} = \text{Dose}_{\text{baseline}} - \text{Dose}_{\text{scenario}}$$

Mathematical Invariant: If $\text{Scenario} \equiv \text{Baseline}$, then $\Delta \text{Dose} \equiv 0.0\,\mu\text{g}$ and $\Delta \% \equiv 0.0\%$.

Equation 9: Community Tailpipe Emissions Avoided
$$\Delta \text{CO}_2 = D \cdot \big(\text{EF}_{\text{old}}^{\text{CO}_2} - \text{EF}_{\text{new}}^{\text{CO}_2}\big) \quad [\text{g CO}_2/\text{day}]$$ $$\Delta \text{PM}_{2.5} = D \cdot \big(\text{EF}_{\text{old}}^{\text{PM}} - \text{EF}_{\text{new}}^{\text{PM}}\big) \quad [\text{mg PM}_{2.5}/\text{day}]$$
D: Commute round-trip distance (km)
EF_old, EF_new: ARAI/CPCB tailpipe emission factors (e.g. 72 g/km bike vs 16 g/km metro)

6. Relatable Communication Units & Mandatory Caveat

Mandatory Scientific Caveat (Part 3.4 & SCI-06):
Cigarette equivalence is derived using Berkeley Earth's empirical conversion rule (22 µg/m³ 24h average PM2.5 ≈ 1 cigarette). This comparison is deployed strictly as an intuitive risk communication metaphor for the public. Ambient urban air pollution differs fundamentally from directly inhaled cigarette smoke in chemical speciation, absence of heavy tobacco pyrolysis products, particle morphology, and physiological deposition mechanics. It must never be presented as a medical diagnosis or clinical prognosis.
